Email rules: CASL and CAN-SPAM

What RadiusPilot does to help you follow anti-spam law, and what stays your job.

1 min readUpdated October 26, 2026

Not legal advice

This is general information. Ask a lawyer about your own outreach.

What RadiusPilot adds to every outreach email

  • Your name and mailing address, and the line “Don’t want emails like this? Reply “unsubscribe” and we won’t email you again.”
  • A List-Unsubscribe header when the email goes out right away
  • A block on every address in your opt-out list
  • A stop at your daily limit

Emails to your clients are left as they are. For outreach the line can’t be switched off: it’s added even when a template has its own unsubscribe link.

What stays your job

  • Put your real mailing address in Settings → Email: the one there at first is an example
  • Add everyone who asks to stop to the opt-out list
  • Write to businesses about things that relate to their work
  • Keep a record of where you found each address: when you use one from Published contacts, the page it came from and the date are saved with it

Canada: CASL

Canada’s anti-spam law applies to commercial email received in Canada, wherever the sender is. You need consent. Implied consent can cover an address a business publishes conspicuously, without a statement that it doesn’t want unsolicited email, when your message relates to the person’s role or business. Guessed or harvested addresses don’t have it.

United States: CAN-SPAM

CAN-SPAM lets you email businesses without prior consent, as long as the email isn’t misleading, says it’s an ad when it is one, includes a valid postal address and offers an opt-out that you honour within 10 business days.

Other places

Rules differ elsewhere. Europe, for one, is much stricter. Check before you write to businesses outside Canada and the United States.
