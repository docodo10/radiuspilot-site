Privacy

Privacy policy

How RadiusPilot handles personal information on this website and in the Windows app.

DraftLast updated October 25, 2026

This is a draft, not yet reviewed by a lawyer

It describes what RadiusPilot does today, but it hasn’t been reviewed yet for Québec’s Law 25, PIPEDA and CASL. Parts highlighted like this are still to be filled in. It will be final before RadiusPilot is released.

Who we are

RadiusPilot is published by the legal name of the business, its postal address. The person in charge of the protection of personal information is name and title, reachable at email address.

What this policy covers

This website and the RadiusPilot app for Windows. It doesn’t cover the websites and services the app connects to, which have their own policies.

This website

  • It sets no cookies and runs no analytics or advertising scripts.
  • It is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may record technical information about visits, such as IP addresses, for security, under the GitHub privacy statement.
  • The live demo stores its sample workspace in your browser’s local storage, on your device. It contains no personal information about you, and you can clear it by clearing this site’s data in your browser.

The app

The app stores your account, your workspaces and your settings on your computer. We don’t receive them: there is no RadiusPilot server yet. Your account password is kept as a salted hash, and your mailbox password is encrypted by Windows for your Windows account.

To do its work, the app sends some information to third-party services, only when you use the feature that needs it:

ServiceWhat it receives, and when
Photon (OpenStreetMap search)What you type in the place search of a new scan
Overpass API (OpenStreetMap)The area and the kinds of businesses of each scan
Each business’s websiteOne ordinary page request during a scan, and when you run a tool on it
Google PageSpeedThe address you check with Site speed
Cloudflare DNS, with Google DNS as a backupThe domain you check with Email health
Internet ArchiveThe domain you check with Site history
Your mail providerYour email, once you connect your mailbox

The app has no analytics, no tracking and no crash reporting.

Information about businesses and the people who run them

Scans list businesses from OpenStreetMap, which is public data under the Open Database License. The Published contacts tool shows only what a business publishes on its own website, one business at a time and at your request, with the page each item comes from. It doesn’t guess addresses or decode hidden ones.

You decide what you do with that information, and you’re responsible for following the law when you contact anyone, including Canada’s anti-spam law (CASL) and private-sector privacy laws.

Email

When you connect your mailbox, your email travels between the app and your mail provider. We don’t see it. Every outreach email carries your mailing address and a way to opt out, and the addresses on your opt-out list are blocked.

When we start keeping data

Cloud accounts and sync are planned, on servers in Canada. Before any of your data is stored there, this policy will be updated to say what is stored, why, for how long and who can see it, and you’ll be asked to agree.

Your rights

You can ask to access the personal information we hold about you, to correct it, to withdraw your consent, to have it deleted, or to receive it in a common format. Write to email address. We’ll answer within 30 days.

You can also complain to the Commission d’accès à l’information du Québec or to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

Children

RadiusPilot is a tool for businesses and isn’t meant for children.

Changes to this policy

We’ll post any change on this page with its date, and say so in the changelog when it matters.
